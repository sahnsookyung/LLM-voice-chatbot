# Ari: VRoid character implementation

## What ships

Use **Character → Open character studio** (Command–Shift–A), then **Appearance → VRoid · refined face**. Portrait framing compares the animated model with the approved illustration. Entire-body framing gives gestures the full stage. The floating character has separate Appearance (**VRoid 3D**) and Framing preferences. Illustration remains the default.

The runtime uses `characters/ari/model/ari-vroid-v9.vrm`. Its editable base remains `ari-vroid-v6.vroid`, with untouched export `ari-vroid-v6-raw.vrm`; there is no separate v9 VRoid project. Run `node scripts/fit_ari_v5.mjs`, `node scripts/finish_ari_v6.mjs`, `node scripts/detail_ari_v7.mjs`, `node scripts/refine_ari_v8.mjs`, then `node scripts/refine_ari_v9.mjs` to reproduce the finished model. The scripts pin their source hashes, so a new VRoid export needs a new fit review. V7–V9 shape and paint changes are baked into the VRM and preserved alongside their source textures and deterministic script. Earlier versions remain available for rollback.

For editor comparison, open `ari-vroid-v6.vroid` in VRoid Studio and the finished v9 in Character Studio. The v6 editor does not contain the post-export refinements. [VRoid's format documentation](https://vroid.pixiv.help/hc/en-us/articles/23731198070809-About-file-formats-used-by-VRoid-Studio) explicitly excludes importing VRM files for editing; renaming v9 to `.vroid` cannot create an editable project. Recreating those changes in VRoid controls/textures would be a separate authoring task. The native Character page and Studio likeness notes identify the versions used.

The model has 54 mapped humanoid bones including individual fingers, facial expressions and 10 hair spring chains (plus five auxiliary chains). The renderer preserves authored MToon materials and uses the official MIT-licensed `@pixiv/three-vrm` loader. Runtime optimization removes unused vertices, combines facial morphs and combines skeleton buffers. VRoid and Blender do not run during playback; no MCP or new inference model is needed.

## Movement and expressions

The existing continuous motion controller now drives the normalized VRM humanoid rig and expression manager. Neutral starts with relaxed, lowered arms and a slight finger curl. Wave, nod, explain, weight shift and a small step blend in and out. V8 adds a controlled hair sway, a full 360-degree twirl with alternating foot lifts, a bilateral arm raise, a shoulder-and-palms shrug, and a point with the index extended and the other fingers curled. Gestures ease in and out and can interrupt one another; a cancelled turn returns smoothly to the nearest forward orientation. Full-body framing leaves room for outstretched arms and raised hands. Randomized blinks, restrained gaze, head motion, breathing and VRM hair springs supply idle movement. Two-bone leg IK preserves foot targets and orientation through weight shifts and the small step. This is in-place acting, not walking or locomotion.

Custom expressions separately blend brow, eye and mouth-corner morphs; surprise uses its VRM preset, and sleepy uses partial eyelid closure. A UV-masked cheek tint blends with embarrassment and happiness, without an extra draw call. Critically damped weights preserve velocity when a new emotion interrupts the previous one. The eight illustration portraits remain design guides, not eight separate 3D meshes. Audio amplitude drives the `aa` mouth expression with smoothing and a stale-audio timeout. This is not phoneme-aligned lip synchronization. Reduced Motion settles gestures and gaze and resets and freezes spring simulation while retaining speech and emotional expression.

V9 separates reach, hold and recovery with quintic easing. Torso/head orientation leads the pointing arm; elbows, wrists and fingers follow with distinct delays. Left/right arm timing is slightly staggered. A critically damped pose response preserves velocity on interruption. Hands use an individual finger-curl cascade, small spreads, thumb articulation, relaxed wrists and restrained idle variation. The **Movement speed** slider (0.65–1.50×) changes gesture tempo. The movement rehearsal stretches its cue durations at slow tempos so it does not cut off a twirl.

## Likeness status

**Ari v9 is implemented and awaiting user visual review.** V4 was rejected for looking too fierce. V5 relaxed the brow and lid angles together, restored lower-cheek volume, rounded the chin, softened the lips and replaced the heavy contour atlas. V6 retains those changes and removes the painted red nose stripe with a localized atlas edit. The original approved portrait remains the neutral comparison target. Everyday cosmetic blush remains in the texture; additional emotional blush is controlled independently at runtime.

The body now has increased bust projection and upper-hip width, with a narrower waist. A fitted charcoal ribbed top and cream cardigan are separate garment templates. The raw export had visible shirt/cardigan intersections at the chest and pockets. Offline smooth deformation fits the front cardigan over the shirt and tapers the torso/waist consistently across skin and clothing. Normals are transformed with the inverse-transpose deformation Jacobian. It adds no vertices, draw calls or per-frame work. The final fit lives in the VRM, not the editable VRoid slider state; preserve both files and the script.

This is a stylistic hourglass direction, not a universal ideal or an anatomical measurement inferred from the covered reference. The [Sewing & Craft Alliance guide](https://www.sewing.org/files/guidelines/10_105_body_types.pdf) describes this silhouette through a defined waist and balanced bust/hips. VRoid supports [layered garments](https://vroid.pixiv.help/hc/en-us/articles/4405497325721-How-do-I-overlay-outfits) and a [texture editor](https://vroid.pixiv.help/hc/en-us/articles/4405430561817-How-to-use-the-Texture-Editor); the fit exceeded this template’s available local controls, so the final adjustment is baked into the exported mesh.

V6 extends the lower hair by up to 15.3 cm while preserving the roots/fringe and authored waves. It updates 50 hair rest nodes and their inverse bind matrices, retains the original rest skin deformation, and increases spring damping. A front/back offset keeps the longer locks away from the torso. Broad emissive highlights are reduced. A skinned ribbed mock-neck overlaps the shirt edge and follows the neck, with geometry fitted outside the skin. This adds 1,152 triangles and reuses the existing material/texture. The first collar attempt exposed a gap and was too tall; the shipped version is shorter, overlaps the shirt and passes a neck-clearance regression.

V7 treats detail as a combination of contour, visibility and paint. The four ear islands are turned toward the viewer and narrowed, with hair swept behind the temples. New ear shading and small gold hoops clarify the helix, concha and lobe. Brows have longer tapered tails without steepening their angle. A bounded lower-face deformation makes the chin slightly lower and narrower while retaining the fuller cheeks. The same deformation is applied to every facial morph endpoint and its normal, so expressions do not return to the old silhouette. Smoke-gray radial iris textures, restrained nose-tip/nostril detail and softer lip highlights replace the earlier flat shading.

The cardigan now has fine knit, ribbed cuffs/hem/placket, pocket stitching and fold shading. Its original alpha map is preserved byte-for-byte; the new opaque color atlas occupies the standard MToon shade map. A small runtime shader hook reuses that sample for the lit color, without an additional texture fetch or per-frame upload. Other VRM viewers will show the color primarily in shaded areas unless they implement the same hook. The hoops are attached to the head joint; no additional animation rig is needed.

The improved authoring order is: compare the same neutral face from front and three-quarter views; correct feature contour and hair occlusion; then add texture detail; finally rehearse expressions, speech and head movement. Eyebrow length must not become angry brow tilt, and ear visibility must not become oversized ears. The first v7 ear attempt was too wide and the hoops sat behind the jaw; both were corrected after native rendering.

V8 reduces the overly prominent v7 ears, shortens the outer rim and lobe, and tucks them closer to the head. It preserves the inner painted contour and applies the deformation to every facial morph. The gray iris has a restrained teal-silver tint, radial detail and small catchlights. The broad hair locks are rebuilt as 176 smooth, fine sections with narrow tapered tips; the new graphite fiber atlas replaces the broad emissive highlight band. Mipmap filtering avoids portrait-size shimmer. More compliant tips and firmer roots provide follow-through. Hair springs use world-space inertia so whole-body twirls do not cancel their lag.

Hair contact has two levels: seven fitted VRM capsules replace the undersized body/clothing collision group, and nine bone-following ellipsoids constrain visible hair vertices after skinning. The latter covers the gaps between sparse spring joints without scanning hair vertices on the CPU each frame. It adds no draw calls. These are padded clothing proxies, not mesh-accurate cloth or strand self-collision; extreme contact may still require shape tuning. The small lower-back cardigan expansion fixes an intersection exposed by the full turn.

V9 exposes slightly more ear by moving the adjacent temple curtain backward by at most 16 mm and inward by at most 7 mm. Ear size, facial morphs, clothing, textures, skin weights and topology remain unchanged from v8. The deformation fades into the crown and preserves the long ends.

**Likeness remains incomplete.** The rebuilt hair is finer and smoother, but its symmetric fringe, volume distribution and relatively straight front sections still differ from the reference's asymmetric painted waves. Facial structure and garment drape remain simpler. Art acceptance is separate from passing animation tests.

## Lightweight hair dynamics

The implementation uses [VRM spring bones](https://vrm.dev/en/vrm1/springbone/) and three-vrm's [spring-joint solver](https://pixiv.github.io/three-vrm/docs/classes/three-vrm.VRMSpringBoneJoint.html). Ten guide chains contain 40 simulated hair joints; the 66,304 hair vertices follow them through skinning. This is a deliberate approximation rather than strand-by-strand dynamics. World-space tail history lets head/body acceleration and rotation excite the hair; faster twirls produce more lag and outward swing without an extra hand-authored fanning pose.

Pose and springs advance together at fixed 1/60-second steps, independent of drawing cadence. Retuning accounts for three-vrm 3.5.5 applying drag per step and stiffness/gravity times dt: damping is converted from 30 Hz, stiffness and gravity are halved, and distal hair response is softened slightly. Catch-up is limited to six steps after a stall, and hidden/resumed windows reset clock debt. The fitted capsules remain, while the final vertex contact uses a smooth shell around each ellipsoid, including a fitted head boundary for scalp coverage when springs are frozen to soften entry into contact. There is no hair-to-hair collision or deforming cloth solver. This trades physical completeness for predictable cost and stable stylized motion.

## Rehearsal controls

The Studio offers shuffled expressions (all eight once), a 12-cue conversation arc, every directed transition (57 cues covering all 56 ordered pairs), rapid 0.4-second interruptions, a custom list of 2–64 emotion names, and a six-cue movement-and-hair rehearsal covering every new gesture. Custom lists preserve repetitions. Pace, gestures and simulated speech can be combined. Pause/resume, Next, Stop and manual emotion selection support interruption review. Hidden or occluded windows pause the sequence without catching up when resumed. Standalone mouth testing pauses the sequence.

## Resource controls and verification

The export has 139,663 mesh triangles, 16 materials, 107 exported rig bones (54 standardized humanoid mappings plus hair/auxiliary bones), and is approximately 26.6 MiB. The native renderer measured 25 draw calls and 163,466 rendered triangles including outline passes. The build bundles the model bytes into its classic script for WKWebView file-origin compatibility. Embedded texture decoding uses only local data/blob URLs; CSP does not allow HTTP connections.

WebGL is created only in 3D mode, capped at 1.5 device pixel ratio, with up to 60 FPS during gestures and their settling tail, and 30 FPS when idle, without shadows or postprocessing. Hidden characters pause; returning to Illustration disposes geometry, materials, textures, skeleton buffers, canvas and WebGL context. The illustration has no continuous animation loop. The VRM enlarges the classic JavaScript bundle to about 38.6 MB; avoiding that base64 payload in illustration-only windows would require a separate native asset delivery path. The v9 native 700×1100 gesture check measured approximately 55–59 FPS over six-second windows, including brief idle tails and pose snapshots. Sampled p95 CPU time was approximately 2 ms per simulation step and 2 ms per draw submission; submission time does not measure GPU execution. Slow/fast twirls produced peak hair-tip radii of approximately 0.30/0.38 m from the turn axis. These values are model-specific checks, not physically calibrated hair measurements. This is a short renderer check; whole-app memory, sustained thermal cost and multitasking responsiveness have not been benchmarked for this model.

```sh
npm --prefix renderer test
bash scripts/build.sh
swift scripts/check_renderer.swift 'build/Local Companion.app/Contents/Resources/renderer/studio.html'
```

Forty-four tests cover the VRM asset contract, finger hierarchy, expression bindings, skin weights, spring references, preservation of authored data during offline tailoring, neutral torso/neck garment clearance, hair rest skinning, continuous motion, all expression pairs, interruption settling, frame-rate independence and foot IK. V7 regressions check all 57 posed facial normals, preserved body fit and cutout alpha, ear/chin/brow changes, new hair rest skinning and head-bound accessories. V8 regressions cover smaller ears, fine outward hair geometry/tips, fitted spring collider references, filtered textures and bounded interruptible gestures. The native WKWebView check covers texture/model loading, actual draws, hidden pause, portrait resume, simultaneous emotional/speech morph weights, cheek tint, planted feet, hair speed settling, actual gesture poses, limb framing and canvas disposal. The standalone harness can save native pose snapshots: `swift scripts/check_renderer.swift 'build/Local Companion.app/Contents/Resources/renderer/index.html' reports/v9-motion`. V9 regressions add fixed-step callback-rate invariance, bounded catch-up, adaptive drawing, local ear exposure, delayed hand articulation, pose-velocity continuity and tempo changes. The native check also compares slow/fast twirl hair extension, records CPU submission timings and verifies reduced-motion hair freezing. Performance regressions check changed ancestor transforms while preventing leg IK from walking unrelated hair/mesh branches, and protect drawing against millisecond callback jitter without changing simulation time or exceeding the frame cap. Native UI review checks front/profile views and the visible likeness differences. These checks do not constitute art approval.

### Performance review, 2026-10-13

The current optimization preserves the v9 VRM byte-for-byte, the 60 Hz simulation, all nine contact volumes, image quality and frame caps. Leg IK updates its own branch and ancestors instead of traversing the entire model twice per step. Drawing shares one explicit scene-matrix update between hair-contact uniforms and WebGL submission. Studio readiness checks use `characterStatus(false)`, avoiding diagnostic geometry queries and percentile sorting every 50 ms. Full diagnostic status remains available to tests.

`scripts/benchmark_renderer.swift` runs a seeded, local WKWebView at the overlay's 300×540 logical-pixel size: warm-up, ten seconds idle, then ten seconds of repeated twirls. The `?profile=1` flag enables cumulative component timings; normal app windows do not collect those extra timings. The baseline and final logs are `reports/renderer-performance-before.txt` and `reports/renderer-performance-final.txt`; intermediate runs are also retained.

| CPU measurement | Before | Final |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Idle pose work / simulation step | 0.448 ms | 0.224 ms |
| Twirl pose work / simulation step | 0.463 ms | 0.173 ms |
| Idle total simulation / step | 0.951 ms | 0.908 ms |
| Twirl total simulation / step | 0.987 ms | 0.878 ms |
| Idle draw submission / frame | 1.006 ms | 1.289 ms |
| Twirl draw submission / frame | 1.030 ms | 1.181 ms |

Both runs maintained approximately 30 idle / 60 active FPS, 60 simulation steps/s, 25 draws and 163,466 rendered triangles. Pose CPU work improved substantially, but the complete CPU frame budget did not show a reliable reduction: work shifted into rig/draw preparation and submission timings varied. These results do **not** establish battery savings, a GPU speedup, or better whole-app latency. CPU clocks have coarse resolution; the lightweight status loop fell from 0.080 ms/call to 0.002 ms/call in the final run.

A repeat before the clock correction (`renderer-performance-confirm.txt`) exposed active drawing at 49.4 FPS despite 60 simulation steps/s and sub-millisecond mean simulation work. A deterministic reproduction with 16/17/17 ms callbacks showed the strict presentation threshold discarding frames: 400 draws in ten seconds despite 600 available callbacks. The corrected limiter allows 1 ms of presentation jitter and retains the small timing debt. That case now draws all 600 frames; a separate long-run jitter test verifies it cannot exceed its cap. The physics accumulator is unchanged. This is a frame-pacing correction, not permission to increase the 60 FPS cap.

The final native benchmark measured 29.98 idle / 59.89 active FPS with 59.98 simulation steps/s during twirls. The reliable improvement is resistance to presentation jitter; larger battery/memory improvements remain the work below.

Next work, in priority order:

1. **Measure sustained concurrent use:** profile voice inference plus the visible overlay while using browser/editor/terminals; record cold/warm reply latency, process memory, missed frames and thermal/power behavior. Previous backend reports are historical and do not measure the new avatar running alongside inference.
2. **Separate model delivery from JavaScript:** the 27.9 MB VRM expands into the 38.6 MB classic script, even in illustration-only windows. A narrowly scoped local binary asset handler or lazy classic-script asset can avoid unnecessary parsing/decoding and transient copies. Verify file-origin restrictions, allowlisted paths, cancellation and GPU cleanup before switching. No new network permission is needed.
3. **Profile the hair GPU path:** hair contributes 111,552 of 139,663 mesh triangles, and contact performs two passes across nine volumes per hair vertex. Evaluate an overlay-specific LOD by reducing along-lock samples while preserving the 176 fine sections, tips, silhouette and spring weights. Keep full detail for close portraits. Do not remove scalp/body contact to gain speed; compare identical poses and transitions first.
4. **Offer an explicit low-power graphics option if needed:** a lower pixel ratio or 30 FPS drawing during gestures can reduce rendering work while preserving 60 Hz hair simulation. This trades visual quality/smoothness, so it should be user-selectable rather than silently applied.

Avoid running a visible large Studio preview alongside the overlay during normal work. Hidden windows already pause; a future Studio-close cleanup could also release its retained WebGL resources while preserving controls for reopening. Blender and VRoid are authoring tools and are not required for playback.

## Replacement workflow investigation (2026-10-13)

- [VRoid Studio](https://vroid.com/en/studio) is free to use on macOS and supplies an anime-oriented parametric base. It is proprietary, and [its preset assets are not CC0](https://vroid.com/en/studio/guidelines). It is a stronger starting point for an editable anime rig, but it does not automatically recreate Ari’s exact face or painterly hair.
- [Blender VRM add-on](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/vrm/) provides VRM import/export, humanoid mapping, MToon materials and Python automation. This is an authoring/export tool, not a source of automatic likeness. No MCP is required.
- [Meshy multi-view image-to-3D](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/webapp/image-to-3d) accepts consistent front/side/back references and supports an A-pose for downstream rigging. Its advertised reconstruction capability still needs visual testing; no purchase or upload has been made.
- [TRELLIS.2](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2) releases model/code under MIT, but its official prerequisites are Linux, CUDA and an NVIDIA GPU with at least 24 GB VRAM. It is not a supported local M4 authoring pipeline.
- Tencent’s [official Hunyuan3D-2.1 demo](https://huggingface.co/spaces/tencent/Hunyuan3D-2.1) uses the [Tencent Hunyuan community license](https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hunyuan3D-2.1), not MIT. The user explicitly approved uploading only `a-pose-v1.png` to this destination. The documented Gradio API accepted that image after the browser file chooser failed. The textured endpoint rejected its 270-second GPU allocation as exceeding the available allowance. The separate geometry-only endpoint succeeded: 30 steps, seed 1234, octree resolution 512, 1,172,808 reported faces, about 61 seconds of server processing. Its original GLB, provenance and Blender inspection renders are in `model/hunyuan-shape-v1/`. It is untextured and unrigged, and has fused fingers, weak facial structure and an unwanted floor plane. It has not replaced the app's artwork or passed likeness review. The textured endpoint failure is recorded in `model/hunyuan-candidate-v1/generation.json`.

The new `design/a-pose-v1.png` and its saved prompt retain Ari’s appearance while separating hands from clothes for reconstruction and rigging. The production acceptance criterion is a side-by-side visual likeness review at matched framing, including front and three-quarter views, followed by correct hands, eye closure, lip articulation and shoulder/wrist deformation. A geometry budget is chosen after the likeness succeeds, with measured runtime cost.

## Local Hunyuan3D on this Mac

The earlier CUDA-only assessment was incomplete. Tencent's [upstream README](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2.1) mentions macOS, but its tested installation uses CUDA PyTorch and the texture rasterizer contains CUDA extensions. Its published VRAM figures are 10 GB for geometry, 21 GB for texturing and 29 GB for the combined pipeline; these are not measurements of Apple unified memory.

The community [dgrauet MLX port](https://github.com/dgrauet/Hunyuan3D-2.1-mlx) implements both stages on Apple Silicon. Its author reports shape-generation peaks of approximately 10 GB at FP16, 6 GB at INT8 and 4 GB at INT4, and approximately nine minutes for a six-view, 512-pixel texture pass on an M2 Pro. The paint stage uses FP16. Those are upstream claims, not benchmarks performed on this M4 Pro. Source revision `5fe21945b790fbb7fb28c510e89babd7b9feabe6` was downloaded and inspected; the MLX pipeline has not been installed or run here.

For this 48 GB Mac, start a local quality baseline with FP16, unloading the voice/chat models during generation. Try INT8 only if measured memory pressure requires it; INT4 is not the default for a likeness-sensitive asset. Generation is an authoring job and does not need to remain loaded during character playback. Generated meshes still need topology cleanup, separate articulating eyes/mouth, hand repair, skin weights and facial shapes.

Storage is the immediate constraint: approximately 26 GiB was free at inspection. The [preconverted weight repository](https://huggingface.co/dgrauet/hunyuan3d-2.1-mlx/tree/main) totals 15.1 GB; the paint loader selects approximately 6.4 GB of weights, plus a 67 MB super-resolution model, while a full environment and conversion caches need additional space. No model weights were downloaded during this investigation. A second [MPS/ROCm community port](https://github.com/VladimirTalyzin/hunyuan3d-2.1-mac-rocm) also advertises Mac geometry and PBR texturing, but has not been inspected or tested locally.
